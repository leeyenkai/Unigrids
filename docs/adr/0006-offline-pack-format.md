# 6. Offline packs are WebP tiles in MBTiles, clipped to the park

Date: 2026-10-18

## Status

Accepted

## Context

`SPECIFICATION.md` §8 says every national park and a curated set of busy state parks get
an offline pack bundled with the app, and §16 calls measuring one the pilot's riskiest
step: everything has to fit the App Store's 4 GB limit, and nobody knew what a pack
weighed.

Zion's pack has now been built and measured
(`Pipeline/out/pack-size-report.md`, regenerated by `unigrids-pipeline build`). Four
things the measurement decided, each of which was an open choice before it:

**Format.** A park tile is shaded relief: continuous tone, no flat areas, which is the
thing PNG compresses worst. Measured on the same tiles across zoom 10–16, Zion's average
tile is 50 KB as PNG, 35 KB as a 256-colour PNG, and 6 KB as WebP at quality 82 — eight
times smaller, at a difference that does not survive putting the two side by side (PSNR
37 dB; the trail dashes and road casings stay crisp). Across 63 parks that is the
difference between 148 GB and 12 GB.

**Container.** Five thousand tiles per park, times 63 parks, is a quarter of a million
files for the build system to walk and sign, each rounded up to a filesystem block.

**Extent.** A park is not its bounding box. Zion's outline is two canyons joined at a
narrow waist, and clipping the tile set to the boundary plus a 2 km buffer costs 47 %
fewer tiles at zoom 16 than the box would.

**Depth.** Zoom 16 is 1.9 m per pixel. 3DEP's national coverage is 10 m, so that zoom's
relief is interpolated — and it is nine tenths of the tiles. Worse, the tile count is
dominated by six Alaskan park-and-preserve units: Wrangell–St. Elias alone encloses a
hundred times Zion's area, almost all of it roadless wilderness with no trail network to
draw at two metres a pixel.

## Decision

A pack is a directory per park under `Pipeline/out/packs/<place-id>/`:

- **`tiles.mbtiles`** — MBTiles 1.3, read-only SQLite, 256 px raster tiles, **WebP at
  quality 82**. MBTiles numbers rows from the south and `MKTileOverlay` asks from the
  north, so the pack's manifest states the flip rather than leaving the app to find it.
- **`trails.json`** — trails measured by the pipeline, with the method recorded next to
  the numbers.
- **`manifest.json`** — extent, zoom range, per-zoom tile counts and bytes, the version of
  every source, and what the pack does *not* contain yet.
- **`ATTRIBUTION.md`** — what the pack owes each source.

Tiles are chosen by **clipping to the park boundary plus a 2 km buffer**, not by bounding
box, and the deepest zoom a park gets **depends on its area**: zoom 16 up to 2,000 km²,
zoom 15 up to 10,000 km², and shallower above that.

Tiles are **relief-shaded terrain, not contour lines**: that is what an NPS brochure map
uses, and contours would be the most expensive thing on the tile to encode. The style
echoes NPS cartography and carries no arrowhead and no seal (`AGENTS.md` §5).

## Consequences

Good: the bundle fits. 63 parks come to 2.3 GB with the tiering above, against 12.4 GB for
uniform full-depth WebP and 149 GB for PNG. A busy state park of 25 km² costs about 2 MB,
so a curated list of a hundred is another 200 MB — the state-park question (§17,
follow-up 1) is not a budget question after all.

Costs, accepted:

- **WebP is lossy.** Fine for shaded terrain; if a future pack carries text or a scanned
  brochure panel in a tile, that tile wants a different format.
- **The biggest parks get a shallower map**, and the rule is area, which is a proxy. Death
  Valley is 13,900 km², road-accessible and busy; it lands in the same tier as Gates of
  the Arctic. Trail density or visitation would be the better rule and needs the trails
  fetched nationally first.
- **Zoom 16 is interpolated relief** anywhere 3DEP has only its 10 m national coverage.
  The renderer computes the shading at the elevation model's own resolution and scales it
  up, so it is smooth rather than terraced, but it is not new information.
- **The brochure PDFs are not measured.** At 12 MB a park they are 0.8 GB, which is most
  of the headroom. The `package` step has to measure them before the budget is closed.
- **The app cannot read a pack yet.** There is no tile-overlay screen; that is 1.0.0
  (`docs/mvp-scope.md`), and until then `package` deliberately does not copy packs into
  the app's resources.
