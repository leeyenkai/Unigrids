# Code review and feature audit — 17 November 2026

A review of the app as it stood at `8c2b38d` (0.10.0 in progress: Japan and Norway).
Five read-only reviewers each took one area: Core, Reference and Log; the map, guide
and trail screens; passport, stats, trips and privacy; the Python pipeline; and a
feature and data audit across the three countries. Every finding below was traced
through the code. The fixes were then checked again, tested and committed. This file
records what was fixed and, more usefully, what is still open.

## Where the app stands

Japan's and Norway's national parks match a US park on everything the guide template
shows: outline, written guide, story, sights, chronology, safety and regulations,
climate, credited photographs, the 3D explorer, a passport slot and stats. They also have
what the US lacks: a local name, How this park works, and access lines for island and
Arctic parks. The differences that remain are mostly data the sources do not publish
(monthly visitation abroad; Norway's visitor numbers; Japanese trail grades), and a
few gaps the OpenStreetMap fetch left (below).

## Still open

### Data abroad (fetch, not code)

- **Japan's Shinkansen lines are named by service** ("Nozomi") rather than by line
  ("Tokaido Shinkansen"): in OpenStreetMap the route master itself carries the service
  name. Naming by line would mean reading the track's own names. 17 Japanese parks have
  no huts mapped, and Keramashoto and Iriomote-Ishigaki no named paths.
- Quasi-national park outlines are still Overpass's earlier snapshot.
- **Campsites abroad**, Norway's park leaflets as PDFs, Norway's remaining 21 authorised
  visitor centres, 56 quasi-national park outlines, Japan's park zones and Norway's dated
  restriction areas on the guide map, and Turrutebasen's routes named only "Ukjent".
- Four Norwegian parks have no photographs (Anárjohka, Borgefjell, Dovre,
  Lomsdal-Visten).
